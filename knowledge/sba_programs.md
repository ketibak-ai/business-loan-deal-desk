# SBA Loan Programs

## SBA 7(a) Basics
The SBA 7(a) program is the SBA's main small business loan. A bank or other approved lender makes the loan, and the SBA guarantees part of it: typically 85% for loans of $150,000 or less and 75% for larger loans. The maximum loan amount is $5 million. Uses include working capital, equipment, real estate, refinancing and buying a business. Terms can run up to 10 years for working capital and equipment and up to 25 years for real estate. Program limits, fees and rules change, so check sba.gov for the current rules.

## SBA 7(a) Rates and Fees
SBA 7(a) rates are usually priced over Prime, and the SBA caps the spread a lender can charge. The cap depends on loan size and whether the rate is fixed or variable. Borrowers pay an upfront guarantee fee to the SBA on the guaranteed portion. The fee schedule changes each fiscal year and may be reduced or waived for smaller loans. The lender may also charge packaging and closing costs. Compare offers using APR, which includes these fees.

## Why Banks Use the SBA Guarantee
The guarantee transfers most of the credit loss to the government. The guaranteed share needs very little capital and carries little expected loss, so lenders can approve borrowers with short histories, limited collateral or thin cash flow. If a conventional loan is declined because of collateral shortfall or fewer than two years in business, ask whether the lender would do it as an SBA 7(a) loan.

## SBA 504 Loans
The SBA 504 program finances major fixed assets such as owner-occupied real estate and long-lived equipment. A typical structure is 50% from a bank first mortgage, 40% from a Certified Development Company (CDC) second loan backed by the SBA, and 10% from the borrower. The CDC portion carries a long fixed rate, often 20 or 25 years. The low down payment preserves working capital. The business generally must occupy most of the property.

## SBA Eligibility
To qualify, a business generally must be for-profit, operate in the United States, meet SBA size standards and show that it cannot get credit on reasonable terms elsewhere. Owners of 20% or more usually give personal guarantees. Lenders still underwrite repayment ability, typically looking for DSCR of about 1.15x or more and acceptable owner credit.
