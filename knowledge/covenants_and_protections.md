# Covenants, Guarantees and Borrower Protections

## Financial Covenants
Covenants are promises in the loan agreement. Common financial covenants are a minimum debt service coverage ratio (often 1.20x to 1.25x), a maximum debt to EBITDA (often 3.0x to 4.0x), and minimum liquidity or net worth. Breaking a covenant is a default even if every payment is on time. It can lead to fees, a higher rate or a demand for repayment.

## Covenant Headroom
Set covenants with room to absorb a bad year. Ask for covenants tested annually rather than quarterly, a cure period, and EBITDA definitions that include agreed add-backs such as one-time costs and owner compensation adjustments. A useful check is how far EBITDA could fall before a breach. Less than 20 to 25% headroom is tight.

## Personal Guarantees
A personal guarantee makes the owner personally liable for the loan. Banks commonly require one from owners of 20% or more. You can negotiate a limited guarantee capped at a dollar amount or percentage, guarantees only from active owners, or release of the guarantee once leverage or coverage targets are met.

## Prepayment Penalties
Prepayment penalties compensate the lender when a loan is repaid early. A step-down structure (for example 3%, 2%, then 1%) is mild. Yield maintenance or make-whole clauses can be very expensive when rates fall. Floating-rate loans often need no penalty at all. Ask for no penalty when you repay from operating cash rather than a refinance.

## Collateral and Liens
A blanket lien covers all business assets. Ask to limit the lien to the specific assets financed, to exclude assets needed for other financing, and for release of collateral as the loan is paid down. Lenders advance a share of collateral value: about 75% for real estate, 80% for equipment and 65% on a blend of receivables and inventory.

## Reporting Requirements
Loan agreements require financial statements, tax returns, compliance certificates and sometimes borrowing base reports. Negotiate reporting that matches what you already produce, such as reviewed rather than audited statements. Missing a reporting deadline can be a technical default.
