# How Banks Price Business Loans

## Cost of Funds
A bank's funding cost is its starting point. Floating-rate loans are funded near SOFR, and fixed-rate loans at a matched-maturity rate from the swap or Treasury curve. Banks add a liquidity premium, often around 25 bps, for tying up funding. Prime is usually about 3 percentage points above SOFR, so a loan priced at Prime plus a spread already carries a large margin over the bank's true cost of funds. Ask for a SOFR-based quote to compare.

## Expected Loss
Expected loss is the average credit loss the bank builds into the price: probability of default (PD) x loss given default (LGD) x exposure. PD comes from the bank's internal risk rating, driven by cash flow coverage, leverage, time in business, owner credit and industry. LGD depends on collateral and guarantees. Unsecured loans lose about 45% in default, and well-secured loans much less.

## Capital and the Hurdle Rate
Banks must hold equity capital against every loan. Pricing models charge for it through risk-adjusted return on capital (RAROC). The bank wants after-tax income, divided by the capital the loan uses, to beat a hurdle rate, commonly 12% or more. Most banks that lend to small businesses hold capital near 10% of the loan balance. Capital is the largest single driver of the minimum rate a bank can accept.

## Relationship Pricing
Banks price the whole relationship, not only the loan. Operating deposits are cheap funding, and treasury-management fees (payroll, ACH, wires, cards, lockbox) add income. A bank can accept a lower loan spread when the relationship as a whole still clears its hurdle. That is why moving your operating accounts is often the most valuable thing you can offer. Most banks give credit only for deposits and services you actually move, not promises.

## Pricing Floors
A bank uses several reference rates to decide the lowest rate it can accept on a loan. The loan-only target earns the hurdle on the loan by itself. The relationship floor earns the hurdle once your deposits and services are counted. The cost floor covers funding, expected loss and servicing costs, and banks rarely go below it. Pricing below a floor usually needs senior approval, so a banker with room to move may still need a strong case to go lower.

## Fees and Upfront Costs
Origination fees, unused fees and annual fees are income to the bank and reduce the rate it needs. Closing costs such as legal, appraisal and filing fees usually go to third parties. Fees raise your true cost, so always compare offers by APR, which spreads upfront fees over the life of the loan.

## Small Business versus Commercial Pricing
Banks price small-business loans and commercial loans with different models. Small-business or business-banking loans, typically for companies under about $5 million in revenue, are scored rather than individually rated: a credit scorecard weighs owner credit, time in business and cash flow, assigns a score band, and each band carries a pooled default rate. Capital follows retail rules, and the rate comes from a pricing grid by band and term, so a banker can usually discount only a limited amount, often around 25 to 50 bps, without an exception. Commercial or middle-market loans get an individual risk rating from a credit analyst, deal-specific economic capital, and relationship pricing, which leaves more room to negotiate. The SBA guarantee can sit on top of either.
