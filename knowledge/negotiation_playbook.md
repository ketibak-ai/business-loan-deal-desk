# Loan Negotiation Playbook

## Prepare Before You Negotiate
Know your numbers before the first call: debt service coverage, debt to EBITDA, collateral coverage and the true cost (APR) of each offer. Get at least two written quotes. Decide your walk-away point and your best alternative, such as another lender, an SBA structure or a smaller loan. Banks move most for borrowers who are organized and have real options.

## Negotiate the Whole Deal
Rate is only one term. Fees, covenants, the personal guarantee, the prepayment penalty, the collateral package and the reporting requirements can each be worth as much as a rate cut. Rank what matters most to your business, and trade things that are cheap for you but valuable to the bank.

## Using Deposits and Treasury Services
Offer to move operating deposits and treasury services in exchange for a lower spread or waived fees. Put the size of the relationship in numbers: average balances and annual service fees. Ask the bank to state the concession in the term sheet, and move accounts only after closing terms are agreed in writing.

## Using a Competing Offer
A written competing term sheet is your strongest lever. Share the rate and key terms, and ask the bank to match or beat it. If the competing rate is below what the bank can accept, it will not match on rate. Ask instead for fee waivers, looser covenants or a limited guarantee, or take the better offer.

## Fee Negotiation
Banks often give on fees before rate. Ask to reduce or waive origination fees, cap legal fees, and remove or reduce unused commitment fees on lines you expect to use heavily. Ask for fees to be waived if you bring deposits.

## Timing and Tactics
Negotiate before you are desperate for cash, ideally 60 to 90 days before you need the funds. Keep the conversation focused on your credit strengths. Ask open questions such as "what would it take to get to SOFR plus 250?" Get every agreed term into the written commitment letter before accepting.

## When to Walk Away
Walk away when the true cost exceeds the return the money will earn, when covenants leave too little headroom to survive a bad quarter, or when the lender requires terms you cannot meet, such as unlimited guarantees from passive owners. A smaller loan on good terms can be better than a larger loan on bad terms.
